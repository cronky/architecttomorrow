# Ollie Cronk: voice profile

Reference for drafting or editing anything published under Ollie's name. Read it alongside `voice-samples.md`, which holds thirteen of his pieces from 2011 to 2022. This file describes the voice. The samples contain it. Where the two disagree, the samples win.

## Evidence base

Built on 12 November 2026 from three of Ollie's site repositories: `cronky/cronky-dot-net`, `cronky/architecttomorrow` and `cronky/cronk-advisory`. After removing duplicates that is 203 pieces and about 119,000 words, dated May 2007 to November 2026.

The baseline is 35 articles, 28,087 words, published between June 2015 and November 2022. That is the period before AI drafting tools were widely available. One 2020 piece that was also published on an employer's blog, and carries American spelling, is left out.

## What the voice is

Every quotation below is from the baseline or the older blog.

**He owns his opinions in the first person.** "I believe", "I fear", "Personally I believe", "I would say", "I really strongly believe". The baseline averages 22 first-person words per 1,000. Claims are his claims, made from where he stands.

**He concedes before he argues.** Paragraphs open with "Whilst", "Of course", "And of course", "After all", "Naturally" or "That said". The objection is dealt with first, then the point is made anyway.

**Asides interrupt the thought.** Spaced hyphens and brackets carry second thoughts, qualifications and jokes: "(and budget!)", "(or flood!)", "(who does?)", "(remember those?!)". There are about 16 spaced dashes and 11 bracketed asides per 1,000 words. The brackets are where the humour lives.

**Sentences run on, then stop short.** Long sentences are joined loosely with And, But, So, Also and Or. One in six runs to 35 words or more. They are broken up by fragments that land as a verdict: "All good things surely?", "Leading to a vicious cycle.", "Interesting times."

**The objector gets a voice.** He quotes the pushback he expects, in the words a stakeholder would use: "We'll fix that in the next phase", "This is just how it works around here", "But I shouldn't be doing this in work time…", "What are you talking about EA has always had an Ecosystem?!"

**He coins labels and puts them in quotation marks.** "Project Cult", "PowerPoint Architect", "Ivory Tower", "AlgoBanking", the "basket of remotes problem", the "Switzerland" of architect communities.

**Metaphors are physical and homely.** "in a hole and keep digging", "letting it rust", "paint over the cracks", "walk a mile in someone else's shoes", "a living hell". Bikes, brakes and LEGO turn up as working analogies for controls, governance and architecture.

**He asks questions and means them.** About 7 question marks per 1,000 words. Forty per cent of baseline titles contain a question mark or an exclamation mark: "EAs: are you providing value for money?", "Where are your brakes?", "Apps are dead! Long live seamless experiences!". Headings inside pieces do the same: "Why?", "What else?", "Slow down!"

**He states the limits of what he knows.** "your mileage may vary", "Caveat - I am not saying I get this right all the time (who does?)", "My experiences are of course mostly based on a few days", "not specific to my current or any previous place I've worked".

**Endings open outwards.** A direct question to the reader, a promised follow-up, thanks to a named person, or a shrug: "Hopefully some food for thought anyway...". He does not close by restating the argument.

**Vocabulary is plain and a little emphatic.** Whilst, etc, i.e., "kind of", "pretty much", "tricky", "crucially", "massive", "amazing", "fantastic", "horrible". Slash pairs such as "smoother/quicker" and "horrible/bizarre". Real people are named and thanked.

**Lists are uneven.** When he uses bullets, items differ in length and some carry commentary or a joke. They are working notes more than a designed layout.

## Drafting rules

1. Work from Ollie's notes, voice-note transcript or draft. Keep his sentences. Correct facts, spelling and anything unclear. Leave rhythm alone.
2. Write in the first person. Opinions are his and are marked as his.
3. Leave his spaced hyphens and bracketed asides where he put them. Do not add em dashes. Do not turn his dashes into colons or full stops.
4. Questions are welcome, in titles and headings too.
5. Avoid "isn't X, it's Y", stacked short sentences for effect, the colon reveal, and groups of three unless there are three things.
6. Titles are a plain statement, a question or an exclamation. No "Topic: Subtitle In Title Case".
7. End on a question for readers, a next step or a thank-you. No summarising final line.
8. Never invent anecdotes, names, examples or figures. Mark a gap as `[OLLIE: what happened here?]`.
9. Match the rhythm of the samples. Do not copy their typos, their grammar slips or their dated facts.
10. Do not caricature. Not every paragraph opens with "Whilst", and an exclamation mark appears only where his draft has one.

Rule 10 matters because imitation overshoots. A model asked to write "in Ollie's voice" from nothing will produce a pastiche of the tics above. Editing his words lightly gives a better result than generating new ones in his style.

## Project instructions

Paste this into the writing project's instructions:

```
This project is for editing and drafting writing published under Ollie Cronk's name.

Read voice-profile.md and voice-samples.md before drafting. The samples are the reference for voice and rhythm only: do not reuse their facts, products or job titles as current.

Work from my notes, transcript or draft and keep my sentences where they work. Follow the drafting rules in voice-profile.md. When I give you a finished draft, default to critique: tell me what is weak, unclear or reads as machine-written, quote the line, and let me rewrite it.

Never invent anecdotes, names or figures. Mark gaps as [OLLIE: ...].
```

## Method

Each post was reduced to plain text, with front matter, images, embeds and link URLs removed. Posts that appear in more than one repository were counted once, matched on month and title. Pieces under 250 words were left out of the counts. Bullets, headings and block quotes were excluded from the sentence-length figures.

The repositories hold no podcast transcripts, so the profile covers the written voice only.
