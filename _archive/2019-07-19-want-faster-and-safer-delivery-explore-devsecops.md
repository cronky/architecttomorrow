You probably know the story - **stakeholders want seemingly incompatible things:**

**﻿**The business want **speed to market of new features**, the ops team - **stability and fixes**, risk and security - well they are often so scared of what you want to do they want you to **stop or delay!**

Before I go further ***I am not bashing risk and security people here!*** Far from it - much like how operations squealed before days of DevOps (more on that in a moment) about things being thrown over the fence to them - it shouldn't be a surprise other stakeholders are uncomfortable if they are not part of the party.

### Hang on stop: how did we even get to DevOps??!

For those of you not so familiar with Software Development let me provide a bit of background. For those already au-fait with DevOps feel free to skip this!

![Picture showing separate risk and security development and operations teams and the SDLC and Secure SLDC flow](https://media.licdn.com/dms/image/v2/C4E12AQE7IwGQ8WOQtw/article-inline_image-shrink_1500_2232/article-inline_image-shrink_1500_2232/0/1563553553539?e=1784160000&v=beta&t=r_hmsEfD-It-ef5LpIw_MMAizw7QMv0DnpoC0vtnBa4)

The traditional world of software delivery via Waterfall/V-model and gated processes is not brilliantly suited to the fast paced world we live in today. There is an argument that it continues to makes sense for a mission critical stuff - like building nuclear power stations where you need to make absolutely sure that everything specified has been built. But in most cases the world has moved on (literally in some cases - stakeholders and requirements can massively shift) before the software even gets delivered.

### Agile and DevOps to the rescue?

So to keep up with the pace of change we moved to smaller more iterative ([agile](https://www.agilealliance.org/agile101/)) delivery models. A common agile methodology is [scrum](https://www.agilealliance.org/glossary/scrum/) (see hyperlinked articles for more info).

Looking at t[he diagram below](https://www.shapechef.com/libs/graphics-and-templates/scrum-shapes): "Input from stakeholders" part top left; if its been done right and there is an agile culture across the whole organisation **including risk and security** - then you are already done - everyone is in step....

![Scrum Process from https://www.shapechef.com/libs/graphics-and-templates/scrum-shapes](https://media.licdn.com/dms/image/v2/C4E12AQF9gNTBeGQYJw/article-inline_image-shrink_1000_1488/article-inline_image-shrink_1000_1488/0/1563556432338?e=1784160000&v=beta&t=cvEVqQ79qFI99WWBt6drtAI7PKamXt_Kgv7ybfLWcM4)

... the trouble is not many are. In reality what has often happened is misalignment. Tech teams in some cases aren't great at stakeholder engagement / communications on the topic and/or in other cases the business just doesn't want to know or engage with this "agile thing" or have the patience to understand it and the value gained). The tech team goes off and builds DevOps team(s). [DevOps](https://theagileadmin.com/what-is-devops/) is as it sounds is a combination of Software **Dev**elopment and Technology **Op**erations teams, skills, processes and tech that leads to a new culture and way of working.

![DevOps circles of Dev and Ops intersecting](https://media.licdn.com/dms/image/v2/C4E12AQEw2AScpK97hg/article-inline_image-shrink_400_744/article-inline_image-shrink_400_744/0/1563556271975?e=1784160000&v=beta&t=MufzyN29zlYbseAmFH_baZWQJopzoTj9R43F13IlcP4)

As I said the trouble is many stakeholders aren't in step:

![DevOps circles of Dev and Ops intersecting with dotted arrows to the security team who are out of step and viewed as a bottleneck](https://media.licdn.com/dms/image/v2/C4E12AQE6JpuWBjIVcA/article-inline_image-shrink_1500_2232/article-inline_image-shrink_1500_2232/0/1563557188661?e=1784160000&v=beta&t=wbdZC1SY8MP8JPln42HK_f1rkUc1nlR3HQMv47pbBqY)

And sometimes stakeholders are afraid or in denial about the automation that comes with DevOps. Traditional, manual ways of doing things are eradicated if you are doing this right. This scares some folks - it shouldn't as it actually empowers them and allows them to stay current and focus more on the things that matter in their roles (rather than being slaves to admin and data collection for example).

> Accelerated time to market, agile and automated approaches pose risks that traditional gated approaches are unsuitable for - security and risk thinking needs to be embedded

### And so we get to DevSecOps

(sorry its taken a while!)

So DevSecOps is about collaborating but also making security everyone's responsibility - living and breathing it; not just saying you do security by design - and creating a "[toolchain](https://tech.gsa.gov/guides/understanding_differences_agile_devsecops/)" where risk and security identification and response is built in.

![Dev Sec and Ops overlapping circles with text saying agile, collaborative development and operations also requires continuous risk and security management](https://media.licdn.com/dms/image/v2/C4E12AQEyfIlvNVHI9A/article-inline_image-shrink_1500_2232/article-inline_image-shrink_1500_2232/0/1563557599006?e=1784160000&v=beta&t=3O_zvYb0rjBkfkKeOPc_aDU8O3MwdxqXourRVU6orWk)

And so for me it makes sense that if you are doing iterative development and continuous deployments (for many advanced firms this is now up to several releases a day) you need the support framework and checks and balances to be aligned with this operating model.
